# 30 AI Prompts for Freelancers: Get Clients, Win Projects, Earn More

## SECTION 1: COLD OUTREACH THAT ACTUALLY GETS REPLIES (Prompts 1–7)

---

**PROMPT 1 — The "I Did My Homework" Cold Email**

```
You are an expert cold email copywriter. Write a cold outreach email for a freelance [YOUR SKILL, e.g. web designer] reaching out to [TYPE OF COMPANY, e.g. local restaurants] who have [PROBLEM YOU NOTICED, e.g. outdated websites with no mobile version].

Rules:
- Subject line under 8 words, curiosity-driven
- Opening line references something specific about THEIR business (I'll fill in the blank: [SPECIFIC DETAIL YOU NOTICED])
- One clear pain point, one clear result you deliver
- Call to action = a 15-minute call, not a sale
- Tone: conversational, not corporate
- Length: under 120 words
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Generic cold emails get deleted. This forces the AI to write something that feels researched and personal — the #1 thing that gets replies.

**PRO TIP:** Before sending, visit their website/LinkedIn and fill in [SPECIFIC DETAIL YOU NOTICED] with something real: "I noticed your homepage still loads your 2019 menu PDF" hits harder than any template.

---

**PROMPT 2 — LinkedIn Connection Message (Gets Accepted + Replied)**

```
Write a LinkedIn connection request message from a freelance [YOUR SKILL] to [ROLE, e.g. marketing director at a SaaS company].

Requirements:
- Under 300 characters (LinkedIn limit)
- Don't pitch a service immediately — build curiosity
- Reference something from their recent post or company news: [WHAT THEY POSTED/DID]
- End with an open question that invites a reply
- No emojis, no "I came across your profile"
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** 90% of LinkedIn pitches get ignored because they sell too fast. This positions you as a peer, not a vendor.

**PRO TIP:** Comment on their post FIRST, then send the connection request the next day. Response rate doubles.

---

**PROMPT 3 — Follow-Up Email (After No Reply)**

```
Write a short follow-up email for a freelancer who sent a cold email [X DAYS] ago and got no reply.

Context: I emailed [COMPANY NAME] about [WHAT I OFFERED]. No response.

Rules:
- Don't apologize for following up
- Add new value or a new angle (not just "checking in")
- One sentence that creates mild urgency without being pushy
- Under 60 words
- End with a yes/no question to lower friction
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** "Just checking in" follow-ups are ignored. This gives them a new reason to reply.

**PRO TIP:** Send this exactly 4 business days after the first email. Too soon = annoying. Too late = forgotten.

**PROMPT 4 — Cold DM for Instagram/Twitter**

```
Write a cold DM for a freelance [YOUR SKILL] reaching out to [TYPE OF ACCOUNT, e.g. e-commerce brand with 10k-50k followers] on [PLATFORM].

They have [SPECIFIC PROBLEM YOU SPOTTED, e.g. inconsistent posting schedule, low engagement on product photos].

Rules:
- First sentence cannot start with "I" or "Hi"
- Mention the problem without being rude
- Offer one specific, free insight or tip to build trust
- CTA: invite them to reply, not to buy
- Under 80 words
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** DMs that lead with value get read. DMs that lead with pitches get blocked.

**PRO TIP:** Screenshot the specific problem you're referencing and attach it to the DM. Instantly separates you from every other freelancer DMing them.

**PROMPT 5 — Referral Request Email (To Past Clients)**

```
Write a friendly email asking a past client for referrals.

Context:
- We worked together on: [PROJECT TYPE]
- Result I delivered: [OUTCOME/RESULT]
- Type of client I'm looking for: [IDEAL CLIENT DESCRIPTION]

Rules:
- Warm opener, remind them of the work you did together
- Make the ask easy and specific (who exactly to refer, and how)
- Offer something in return if appropriate: [REFERRAL INCENTIVE OR NONE]
- Casual, not salesy
- Under 150 words
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Referrals are the highest-converting leads. Most freelancers never ask. This removes the awkwardness.

**PRO TIP:** Send this 2-3 weeks after you deliver a successful project, when the client is still excited about the results.

**PROMPT 6 — Re-engagement Email (Dormant Past Client)**

```
Write a re-engagement email to a past client I haven't worked with in [TIMEFRAME, e.g. 6 months].

Client background:
- Industry: [INDUSTRY]
- Last project: [WHAT YOU DID]
- Result: [WHAT HAPPENED]

Current context: I want to offer them [NEW SERVICE OR UPDATE].

Rules:
- Reference the past work to rebuild connection
- Lead with something NEW — a relevant trend, update, or insight about their industry
- Soft pitch the new service as a natural next step
- No desperate energy
- Under 180 words
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Past clients are 5x easier to close than cold leads. This reactivates them without seeming desperate.

**PRO TIP:** Pair this with a relevant article or case study from their industry to make the "new insight" feel genuine.

**PROMPT 7 — The Loom Video Script (For Video Cold Outreach)**

```
Write a 60-second Loom video script for cold outreach from a freelance [YOUR SKILL] to [TARGET COMPANY].

Structure:
1. Hook (5 sec): Name them + the specific problem you spotted
2. Quick credibility (10 sec): One result you've delivered for someone similar
3. Demo/insight (35 sec): Walk them through [SPECIFIC THING YOU'LL SHOW IN THE VIDEO]
4. CTA (10 sec): One clear, low-commitment ask

Tone: confident, conversational, like talking to a friend
No reading from a script (write it to sound natural when spoken)
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Personalized Loom videos have a 3x higher reply rate than text emails. Most freelancers don't use them. You'll stand out immediately.

**PRO TIP:** Record yourself actually on their website/work in the video. The visual proof of your research closes the gap between cold and warm.

## SECTION 2: PROPOSALS THAT WIN PROJECTS (Prompts 8–14)

---

**PROMPT 8 — Winning Project Proposal**

```
Write a freelance project proposal for a [YOUR SKILL] project.

Project brief from client: [PASTE CLIENT'S BRIEF HERE]

My background for this project: [YOUR RELEVANT EXPERIENCE/SKILLS]
My proposed approach: [YOUR METHODOLOGY IN 2-3 SENTENCES]
My estimated timeline: [TIMELINE]
My price: [PRICE OR RANGE]

Structure the proposal as:
1. One-paragraph "I understand your problem" opener
2. My approach (3 bullet points)
3. What you get (deliverables list)
4. Timeline
5. Investment
6. Why me (2-3 sentences, no clichés)
7. Clear next step

Tone: confident, specific, consultative. No fluff.
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Most freelancers send a price and a skills list. A real proposal shows you understand the client's business — that's what wins work.

**PROMPT 9 — Price Justification Script**

```
A client is questioning my rate of [YOUR RATE] for [SERVICE]. They say it "seems high."

Write a confident response that:
1. Doesn't apologize for or lower the price
2. Reframes cost as ROI (what they gain vs. what they spend)
3. Compares to the cost of NOT solving the problem
4. Offers ONE alternative (smaller scope, not lower rate)
5. Stays warm, not defensive

My relevant result I've delivered: [PAST RESULT]
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Price objections are almost always about perceived value, not money. This script reframes the conversation.

**PRO TIP:** Never negotiate rate. Negotiate scope. "I can do X at that budget, which means we'd cut Y" protects your positioning.

```
My best result/case study: [SPECIFIC ACHIEVEMENT, e.g. increased client's conversion rate by 34%]
My unique angle/background: [WHAT MAKES YOU DIFFERENT]

Rules:

- Focus on client outcomes, not your skills list
- Include one specific number or result
- End with a clear call to what the client should do next
```

**PROMPT 11 — Scope Creep Response**

```
A client is asking me to do [EXTRA WORK] that was not included in our original agreement for [ORIGINAL PROJECT].

Write a professional, non-confrontational response that:
1. Acknowledges the request positively
2. Reminds them (gently) of the original scope
3. Presents the extra work as an add-on with a price
4. Makes it easy for them to say yes to the paid add-on
5. Doesn't damage the relationship

My rate for this extra work would be: [RATE]
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Scope creep kills freelancer margins. This script protects your time while keeping the relationship intact.

**PROMPT 12 — Testimonial Request Email**

```
Write an email asking a happy client for a testimonial.

Context:
- Project completed: [PROJECT TYPE]
- Result delivered: [OUTCOME]
- Client seemed pleased because: [WHAT THEY SAID OR SHOWED]

Make the ask easy by providing:
1. 3 optional questions they can answer (not a blank page)
2. A note that a few sentences is enough
3. Where the testimonial will be used: [WEBSITE / LINKEDIN / PORTFOLIO]

Tone: grateful, relaxed, no pressure
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** "Will you write me a testimonial?" gets vague responses. Giving them questions gets you specific, quotable proof.

**PRO TIP:** Send this within 48 hours of the client's most excited reaction — when the result is fresh.

**PROMPT 13 — Project Kickoff Message**

```
Write a project kickoff message to send a new client after signing a contract.

Project type: [PROJECT]
Start date: [DATE]
Key deliverables: [LIST]
What I need from them: [INPUTS REQUIRED]
First milestone: [MILESTONE + DEADLINE]

Tone: professional but warm, builds confidence that they made the right choice

```

**WHAT IT DOES:** A confident kickoff message sets the tone for the entire project. Clients who feel informed give less feedback anxiety and pay faster.

**PROMPT 14 — Late Payment Follow-Up**

```
Write a payment follow-up message for an invoice that is [X DAYS] overdue.

Invoice amount: [AMOUNT]
Original due date: [DATE]
Client relationship: [LONG-TERM / NEW / ONE-TIME]
My tone preference: [FIRM BUT PROFESSIONAL / GENTLE REMINDER]

Requirements:
- Don't apologize for asking
- Include the invoice number and amount clearly
- If [DAYS] > 14, include a note about late fees or pausing work
- Give them a specific deadline to respond
- Keep it short — under 100 words
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Late payment follow-ups feel uncomfortable, so most freelancers wait too long. This removes the emotional charge and gets the money moving.

## SECTION 3: CONTENT & PERSONAL BRAND (Prompts 15–20)

---

**PROMPT 15 — LinkedIn Post That Attracts Clients**

```
Write a LinkedIn post for a freelance [YOUR SKILL] that attracts inbound client leads.

Topic: [A RESULT YOU GOT FOR A CLIENT, A LESSON YOU LEARNED, OR AN INDUSTRY INSIGHT]

Structure:
- First line: Bold hook that stops the scroll (no "I'm excited to share")
- Lines 2-4: Story or insight with specific details
- Lines 5-6: The lesson or takeaway for the reader
- CTA: Invite comments or connections, subtly mention your service

Format: Short paragraphs, no bullet points, conversational voice
Length: 150-250 words
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** LinkedIn algorithm favors posts with high early engagement. A strong hook is the only variable that matters.

---

**PROMPT 16 — Twitter/X Thread (Builds Audience + Attracts Leads)**

```
Write a Twitter/X thread for a freelance [YOUR SKILL] on the topic: [TOPIC]

Thread structure:
- Tweet 1: Bold claim or counterintuitive statement (the hook)
- Tweets 2-7: Expand with specific points, each self-contained
- Tweet 8: Tie it together with the main lesson
- Tweet 9: CTA — follow me if you want more of this / DM me if you need help with [SERVICE]

Each tweet: under 280 characters
No buzzwords. Specific and direct.
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Threads position you as an expert. One good thread can generate dozens of leads over weeks from retweets.

```
- What I did: [YOUR WORK]
- Result achieved: [SPECIFIC OUTCOME WITH NUMBERS IF POSSIBLE]
- Timeline: [HOW LONG]

Format:

- The Challenge: 2-3 sentences
- My Approach: 3-4 bullet points
- The Results: 1-2 sentences with numbers
- What the client said: [QUOTE IF AVAILABLE, or leave placeholder]

Tone: confident, specific, no jargon
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Case studies are the highest-converting portfolio content because they prove results, not just skills.

## SECTION 4: PRICING, PACKAGES & BUSINESS (Prompts 21–26)

---

**PROMPT 21 — Design a Freelance Service Package**

```
Help me create a tiered service package for my freelance [SERVICE].

My current hourly rate: [RATE]
Average project duration: [HOURS/WEEKS]
Common client requests: [LIST 3-4 COMMON ASKS]
What clients value most: [SPEED / QUALITY / ONGOING SUPPORT / OTHER]

Create 3 packages:
- Starter (entry-level, low commitment)
- Core (most popular, best value)
- Premium (done-for-you, fast turnaround)

For each: package name, what's included, price, who it's for, and one reason it's worth the price.

Make the Core package so attractive that it's the obvious choice.
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Packages eliminate hourly rate negotiation. Clients choose between your packages instead of negotiating your rate.

---

**PROMPT 22 — Rate Increase Email (To Existing Clients)**

```
Write a professional email informing a long-term client of a rate increase.

Current rate: [CURRENT RATE]
New rate: [NEW RATE]
Effective date: [DATE — give at least 30 days notice]
Reason (optional to share): [REASON IF ANY]
Client history: We've worked together for [TIMEFRAME] on [TYPES OF PROJECTS]

Rules:
- Lead with appreciation, not apology
- State the new rate clearly, no hedging
- Give them a clear transition date
- Offer to discuss if needed
- Don't over-explain or justify excessively
- Under 150 words
```

**PRO TIP:** Annual rate increases of 10-15% are expected by professional clients. Freelancers who never raise rates get trapped.

**PROMPT 23 — Project Estimate Calculator Prompt**

```
I'm estimating a freelance project. Help me price it correctly.

Project description: [DESCRIBE THE PROJECT]
Client type: [STARTUP / SMB / ENTERPRISE]
My hourly rate: [RATE]
My experience with this type of work: [BEGINNER / INTERMEDIATE / EXPERT]

Calculate:
1. Estimated hours (break down by task)
2. Recommended price (with a 20% buffer for unknowns)
3. Risk factors that could increase scope
4. What to clarify with the client before confirming the price
5. Red flags in the brief (if any)

Be specific. Don't round to nice numbers without justification.
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Underquoting is the #1 freelancer mistake. This forces you to think through every task before committing to a price.

**PROMPT 25 — Income Goal Reverse-Engineer**

```
Help me reverse-engineer my income goal as a freelancer.

My monthly income goal: $[AMOUNT]
My current rate: $[RATE] per [HOUR/PROJECT/MONTH]
How many hours I want to work per week: [HOURS]
Current number of clients: [NUMBER]

Calculate:
1. How many clients/projects I need at my current rate
2. How many hours I need to work
3. If those numbers are realistic — and if not, what needs to change
4. Three specific actions I should take THIS WEEK to move toward the goal
5. What rate I'd need to charge to hit the goal with fewer clients

Be direct. Tell me if my goal is unrealistic with current inputs.
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** Most freelancers don't do the math. This forces clarity on what needs to change.

**PROMPT 26 — "Should I Take This Project?" Decision Framework**

```
Help me decide if I should take this freelance project.

Project details: [DESCRIBE THE PROJECT]
Client type: [WHO THEY ARE]
Budget: [WHAT THEY'RE OFFERING]
Timeline: [DEADLINE]
Red flags (if any): [ANYTHING THAT FELT OFF IN THE CONVERSATION]
My current workload: [AVAILABLE / BUSY / OVERLOADED]
My financial situation: [NEED THE MONEY / STABLE / COMFORTABLE]

Evaluate on:
1. Fair compensation (yes/no + why)
2. Scope risk (is it likely to expand?)
3. Client quality signals
4. Strategic value (portfolio / referral potential)
5. Opportunity cost

Give me a clear recommendation: Take it / Negotiate first / Pass — and tell me why.
```

```
My current projects: [LIST ACTIVE PROJECTS]
```

**PROMPT 28 — Client Onboarding Questionnaire**

```
Create a client onboarding questionnaire for a freelance [YOUR SKILL].

The questionnaire should:
1. Help me understand their business goals (not just the project)
2. Clarify their timeline and budget expectations upfront
3. Identify decision-makers and approval processes
4. Uncover any constraints or past failures with similar projects
5. Get the assets/information I need to start work

Include 10-15 questions.
Mark which are "must answer" vs. "nice to have."
Keep it professional but conversational, not like a government form.
```

**WHAT IT DOES:** A good questionnaire eliminates 80% of project miscommunications before they happen.
